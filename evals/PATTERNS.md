# Cross-case patterns

What gtmstack has actually learned, observed across the three real
multi-book syntheses it's produced so far: Cursor 2026, Anthropic-Brazil
2026, and gtmstack-on-itself 2026.

This isn't a manifesto. It's a field report. Patterns earn their place
here only when they show up in multiple cases.

---

## 1. Convergence is the highest-confidence signal

When two or more frameworks reach the same answer through different
reasoning, the answer is more reliable than either framework's solo
verdict. The syntheses converged on:

| Case | Convergent findings |
|------|---------------------|
| Cursor 2026 | 4 — beachhead identity, wrong-pin-now (F500 IT), parallel-pursuit risk, multipolar-model trend |
| Anthropic-Brazil 2026 | 5 — beachhead identity, category claim, whole-product gap, wrong-pin-now (public sector), parallel-pursuit risk |
| gtmstack 2026 | 5 — demand validation is the bottleneck, convert-one-warm-mention is the next move, capacity is upstream of strategy, niche subcategory is consensus, outbound/monetization are deferred |

The beachhead identity converges in every case. Two or more books,
analyzing different signal slices, name the same buyer at the same
companies in the same words. When that happens, debate about who the
customer is should stop. The data is in.

The wrong-pin-now exclusion converges in every case. Multiple books
reach the same "do NOT chase this segment right now" finding through
different mechanisms. This is the most actionable cross-case pattern:
the "no" emerges with high confidence even when the "yes" is still
ambiguous.

---

## 2. Divergence is the strategic question

Where books disagree isn't framework failure. It's where the operator
still owns the call. Each synthesis flagged 2-3 substantive divergences:

- **Cursor 2026.** Chasm-position (across vs. approaching) and
  category framing (assistant vs. editor). Both are real choices.
- **Anthropic-Brazil 2026.** Magic-triangle company-leg readiness;
  whether Bedrock-mediated distribution permanently positions
  Anthropic as a model option inside someone else's category.
- **gtmstack 2026.** Who the first visionary segment is; how much
  non-author validation is enough to act; whether and on what terms to
  commercialize.

The synthesis section that says "the books diverge here" is doing real
work: it's telling the operator which decisions they cannot escape by
running another framework. Add more books, the convergence broadens.
The divergence shrinks but doesn't go to zero.

---

## 3. Whole-product gaps cluster around 5-6 concrete items

Both Cursor and Anthropic-Brazil syntheses converged on ~5-6 concrete
whole-product items prescribed by all books. The pattern:

- **4-of-4 (or 5-of-5) prescribed** → table-stakes. Ship before any
  sales motion. Examples: Anthropic-Brazil's LGPD attestation,
  Brazilian data residency, BRL invoicing, Portuguese product surface,
  local presence, named in-segment reference.
- **2-of-4 prescribed** → high-priority. Examples: Cursor's
  prompt-logging exports, license-of-generated-code attestations.
- **1-of-4 prescribed** → framework-specific. Useful but not load-
  bearing across the GTM. Re-evaluate when the other constraints relax.

This is the cleanest decision rule the framework produces: count how
many books name a whole-product item, then sequence by count.

## 4. "Parallel pursuit" is the universal failure mode

Every synthesis flagged a version of "you're trying to do too many
things at once":

| Case | What was being pursued in parallel |
|------|------------------------------------|
| Cursor 2026 | Four adjacent product surfaces (code review, refactoring, agent workflows, multi-IDE) |
| Anthropic-Brazil 2026 | Named banks + retailers + telcos + public sector + LATAM expansion + Lusophone Africa |
| gtmstack 2026 | More cases + more rubrics + v2 strict mode + hosted version + outbound |

Different domains, same pattern: the GTM hypothesis names many
adjacent things, and the books reach unanimous "pick one" verdict.
Multi-book synthesis is unusually good at catching this because every
book in the set was *originally written* against this failure mode —
Chasm against four-pin chasing, Dunford against feature-list
positioning, Predictable Revenue against unspecialized roles, Play
Bigger against magic-triangle misalignment, Mom Test against
hypothesis dilution.

If a multi-book synthesis does NOT surface a parallel-pursuit warning,
either the case is unusually focused or the analyst missed the signal.

## 5. Self-diagnostic produces the sharpest verdict

When gtmstack ran on itself (the `gtmstack-2026` case), the per-book
scores were:

| Book | Score |
|-----:|------:|
| Crossing the Chasm | 0.952 |
| Obviously Awesome | 1.000 |
| Predictable Revenue | 1.000 |
| Play Bigger | 1.000 |
| The Mom Test | 1.000 |

And the verdict was: stop building. The author had been about to ship
more rubrics, a v2 strict mode, and a hosted version. All five books
said the same thing in different vocabulary: that work was premature
until one non-friend operator had run gtmstack on a live problem.

This is the framework's epistemic discipline test. A diagnostic tool
that can't produce a verdict its author doesn't want to hear isn't
diagnosing — it's flattering. gtmstack-on-gtmstack produced the
unflattering verdict cleanly.

## 6. The "no" emerges faster than the "yes"

A subtle cross-case pattern. Multi-book synthesis converges on
**exclusions** (wrong-pin-now, deferred motions, structurally bad
segments) with higher confidence than on **the next positive move**.

Why: exclusions are constrained by multiple books' worst-fit criteria
intersecting. The positive move requires choice among several
defensible options.

| Synthesis | Easiest convergence | Hardest divergence |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Cursor 2026 | F500 IT is wrong-pin-now (consensus) | What's the next adjacent product surface (genuine choice) |
| Anthropic-Brazil 2026 | Petrobras / Caixa / SI-mediated banks are wrong-pin-now (consensus) | Bedrock-mediated vs. Anthropic-direct as primary distribution (genuine choice) |
| gtmstack 2026 | Outbound, monetization, hosted-version are deferred (consensus) | Who the first visionary operator is (genuine choice) |

The practical use: when you run a multi-book synthesis and get clear
exclusions plus ambiguous positive moves, that's the framework
working correctly. Stop chasing the consensus next move. Pick from
the divergent options on judgment, knowing the exclusions are real.

---

## What this section does NOT claim

These patterns hold across three cases. Three is not a sample size
that would survive peer review. Calibration cases (Slack 2014, Zoom
2018) are chasm-only, so they don't contribute to cross-book pattern
detection. As the case library grows, expect:

- Patterns 1-2 to hold (they're structural to multi-book synthesis).
- Pattern 3 (whole-product gap counts) to need refinement as more
  cases reveal book-specific quirks.
- Pattern 4 (parallel pursuit) may be partly *selection bias* — the
  cases authored so far are companies where parallel pursuit was
  visibly under discussion. A case where the company has clear focus
  would falsify or confirm.
- Pattern 5 (self-diagnostic sharpness) needs more self-diagnostic
  cases. n=1 today.
- Pattern 6 (faster "no" than "yes") needs more cases to verify.

If you run gtmstack and the patterns above don't hold for your case,
that's data. Append your observation here, or DM me.
